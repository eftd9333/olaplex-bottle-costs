## Start with the bottle, not the brand

Nº.4 Bond Maintenance is the default Olaplex shampoo, and the one the brand itself calls its "#1 salon shampoo & conditioner." It's sulfate-free (no SLS/SLES), color-safe, dermatologist tested and vegan, and it's built for daily washing across all hair types. One practical detail people miss: it's a concentrated formula, and the official usage note is to start with a coin-sized amount and add more only if needed. If you're lathering it like a drugstore shampoo, you're burning through a $34 bottle roughly twice as fast as you need to.

Nº.4C Clarifying is the one to buy if your complaint is buildup rather than dryness. It uses what Olaplex calls a broad-spectrum clarifying system to strip hard-water minerals, chlorine, product residue and excess oil without going the harsh-sulfate route. The official instruction is once every one to two weeks, alternating with your regular wash. If you dye your hair, Olaplex recommends waiting at least two weeks after a color service before using it, which is worth remembering before you build a routine around it.

Nº.4P Blonde Enhancer Toning is the purple one, for blonde, lightened and grey hair that's going brassy. The selling point is adjustable toning: the longer you leave it on, the stronger the effect, with the brand claiming up to three times brighter results than typical purple shampoos. That "three times" number is a brand claim, not an independent test result, so treat it as marketing with a kernel of truth.

Nº.4D Clean Volume Detox is a dry shampoo, not a wash-day product. Rice starch absorbs oil between washes and the formula is aimed at adding volume to the roots. It comes in a 50ml mini and a 250ml full size, and on the EU store that works out to roughly €16 for the mini and €29.50 for the full size.

Then there are the two variants most people never see in their search results: Nº.4 Fine Bond Maintenance, aimed at fine or limp hair that gets weighed down by heavier formulas, and Nº.4 Bond Shaper Hydrating Curl Shampoo, sold in a 1L backbar size for curls. ## The four sizes, and why the 250ml is the worst deal per millilitre

The 250ml bottle is what everyone buys, and it's the most expensive way to buy the product. Run the numbers on Nº.4C: four 250ml bottles at €34 each comes to €136 for a litre of product. The official 1L bottle is €96. That's roughly 29% cheaper for the identical formula, just in a bigger bottle with a pump.

Backbar sizes exist for several formulas, and discount beauty retailers routinely list 1L and even 2L versions well under the per-millilitre price of the small bottle. Two caveats before you go hunting for the cheapest litre online. First, the litre format is designed for salons and it doesn't travel well — decanting into a smaller bottle is normal practice. Second, Olaplex has a long history of counterfeit listings, so an unusually cheap litre from an unfamiliar marketplace seller is a risk, not a bargain.

## Quick answers to the questions this search usually hides

**Is the $34 bottle worth it?** Depends entirely on what you're fixing. If you bleach, heat-style or have genuinely damaged hair, Nº.4 and the occasional Nº.4C are cheap insurance relative to the cost of a color service. If your hair is fine, healthy and your real complaint is greasy roots by day two, Nº.4D or a lighter daily wash is the smarter purchase and you've just saved yourself $34.

**Does the toner replace the regular shampoo?** No. Nº.4P is a toning step. Most routines run Nº.4 for the wash and Nº.4P on a separate schedule for brass.

**Which one for color-treated hair?** Nº.4 is color-safe. With Nº.4C, Olaplex recommends waiting at least two weeks after a color service.

**How long should a 250ml bottle last?** The official dosage is a coin-sized amount per wash, so that's your benchmark. If yours is gone in six weeks, you're using more than the formula calls for.

**Is Amazon cheaper?** Sometimes dramatically, sometimes more expensive than the brand store. Check the seller. Counterfeits are a real category on this brand.

**Does cashback work on discounted orders?** Usually yes, but the rate is calculated on what you actually paid, and the offer's terms may exclude certain promo combinations.
